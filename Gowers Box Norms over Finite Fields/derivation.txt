\subsection{Setup and conventions}
Throughout, $p$ is an odd prime, $G=\mathbb{F}_p^n$, $e_p(t)=\exp(2\pi i t/p)$,
and $\mathbb{E}$ denotes the uniform average. The diagonal derivative and box
norm are
\[
\Delta_{(h,h)}f(x,y)=f(x+h,y+h)\overline{f(x,y)},\qquad
\|g\|_\square^4=\mathbb{E}_{x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2}\,
g(x_1,y_1)\overline{g(x_2,y_1)}\,\overline{g(x_1,y_2)}\,g(x_2,y_2).
\]
The target class under study is the reading-(i) class of $(-1)^{q(x,y)}$,
$\{\,a(x)b(y)c(x+y)e_p(Q(x,y)) : a,b,c \text{ unimodular},\ Q \text{ quadratic}\,\}$.
It is incomparable with the genuinely $\{\pm1\}$-valued reading-(ii) class
$\{a(x)b(y)c(x+y)\sigma : \sigma:G\times G\to\{\pm1\}\}$ for $n\ge2$:
$e_p(t)\in\{\pm1\}$ forces $t\equiv0$ or $t\equiv(p-1)/2$, so generically a
$(\pm1)$-phase lies outside the class, while conversely $e_p(\lambda x_1y_2)$
is excluded from the reading-(ii) class by the swap-asymmetry of its mixed
multiplicative difference; at $n=1$ the reading-(i) class equals
$\{a(x)b(y)c(x+y)\}$, strictly contained in the reading-(ii) class. Every
result below addresses reading (i) only.

\subsection{Step 1: the hypothesis is a spectral mass condition}
Let $B_f(x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2)=f(x_1,y_1)\overline{f(x_2,y_1)}\,
\overline{f(x_1,y_2)}f(x_2,y_2)$. Expanding the box expression of
$\Delta_{(h,h)}f$ term by term, the four shifted factors assemble to
$B_f(z+(h,h,h,h))$ and the four conjugated unshifted factors to
$\overline{B_f(z)}$, so with $u=(h,h,h,h)$,
\[
\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\|_\square^4=\mathbb{E}_{z\in G^4}B_f(z+u)\overline{B_f(z)}
=\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{G^4}}\bigl|\widehat{B_f}(\xi)\bigr|^2 e_p(\xi\cdot u).
\]
Averaging over $h$ retains exactly the characters with
$\xi_1+\xi_2+\xi_3+\xi_4=0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral}
\mathbb{E}_h\,\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\|_\square^4
=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi_0}\bigl|\widehat{B_f}(\xi)\bigr|^2 .
\end{equation}
The left side is nonnegative, and \eqref{eq:spectral} exhibits it as Fourier
$L^2$-mass of $B_f$ on the zero-sum subsystem $\Xi_0$, of codimension $n$ in
$\widehat{G^4}$.

\subsection{Step 2: multiplicative calculus of the class}
For odd $p$, $xy=\bigl((x+y)^2-x^2-y^2\bigr)/2$, whence for $n=1$ every
quadratic $Q(x,y)=Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2+Dx+Ey+F$ splits as
$\alpha(x)+\beta(y)+\gamma(x+y)+\kappa$ with
$\alpha=(A-\tfrac{B}{2})x^2+Dx$, $\beta=(C-\tfrac{B}{2})y^2+Ey$,
$\gamma=\tfrac{B}{2}s^2$, $\kappa=F$: at $n=1$ the phase is redundant. For
$n\ge2$ the monomial $x_1y_2$ obstructs: a mixed finite difference applied to
a purported identity $x_1y_2=\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\kappa$ yields
$\gamma(s+w)-\gamma(s)=w_1s_2$ up to constants, and symmetrizing in $(s,w)$
forces $w_1s_2=s_1w_2$ for all $s,w$ — false at $s=e_2,w=e_1$. Next, against
the box pattern the factors $a(x)b(y)$ cancel pairwise:
\[
\|a(x)b(y)w(x+y)\|_\square^4=\|w\|_{U^2(G)}^4 ,
\]
because $s_{ij}=x_i+y_j$ sweep every additive quadruple exactly once.
Consequently, for $f=a(x)b(y)c(x+y)$,
\[
\mathbb{E}_h\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\|_\square^4
=\mathbb{E}_h\|\Delta_{2h}c\|_{U^2(G)}^4=\|c\|_{U^3(G)}^8 :
\]
inside the target class the hypothesis is precisely a Gowers $U^3$ condition,
and the inverse theorem for $U^3$ over $\mathbb{F}_p^n$ explains every class
member obeying it by a quadratic phase — confirming degree $2$ as the right
target complexity within the class.

\subsection{Step 3: the diagonal family and an equivalent inequality}
Every $f=d(x-y)$ satisfies $\Delta_{(h,h)}f=d(x-y)\overline{d(x-y)}=1$, so the
hypothesis holds with $\delta=1$. Under the bijection
$x=(m+s)/2$, $y=(m-s)/2$, correlation of such $f$ with the class becomes
\[
\Bigl|\;\mathbb{E}_{s,m}\;
d(s)\,\overline{a\bigl(\tfrac{m+s}{2}\bigr)}\,
\overline{b\bigl(\tfrac{m-s}{2}\bigr)}\,\overline{c(m)}\,
e_p\!\bigl(-Q\bigl(\tfrac{m+s}{2},\tfrac{m-s}{2}\bigr)\bigr)\;\Bigr| ;
\]
at $n=1$ the twist absorbs by Step 2, so the maximal-hypothesis case of the
problem is exactly the open problem of a uniform positive lower bound for this
trilinear correlation over all unimodular $d$.

\subsection{Step 4: rigidity of rank-one diagonal increments}
If $f=a(x)b(y)d(x-y)e_p(Q)$ with $Q(z)=\tfrac12 z^{T}Mz+l^{T}z+\kappa$ on
$G\times G$, then $Q(z+\delta)-Q(z)=(M\delta)^{T}z+\tfrac12\delta^{T}M\delta
+l^{T}\delta$ has vanishing mixed $xy$ coefficient, while
$d(x+h-(y+h))\overline{d(x-y)}=1$; hence
\[
\Delta_{(h,h)}f(x,y)
=a(x+h)\overline{a(x)}\,e_p\bigl((Ph)^{T}x\bigr)\cdot
 b(y+h)\overline{b(y)}\,e_p\bigl((Rh)^{T}y\bigr)\cdot e_p(\omega_h),
\]
with $P=M_{xx}+M_{xy}$, $R=M_{yx}+M_{yy}$,
$\omega_h=\tfrac12(h,h)^TM(h,h)+l^T(h,h)$: pointwise rank-one increments.
Multiplication by $e_p(xy^2)$ contributes the mixed term $2hxy$ and destroys
the splitting for every $h\ne0$. The exact converse classification is open,
the defining rank-one increment law being the only currently verified
invariant; the parallelogram equation
$\Theta(u+w,v+t)\Theta(u-w,v-t)=\Theta(u+t,v-w)\Theta(u-t,v+w)$ is NOT
necessary — $f=e_p(x^2)$ and $f=e_p(xy)$ have pointwise rank-one diagonal
increments yet their pulled-back $\Theta$ violates it, as does the plain
constituent $\Theta=d(v)$ — so it cannot serve as the necessity mechanism.
The natural rigidity class $\{a\,b\,d(x-y)e_p(Q)\}$ differs from the
transmitted class $\{a\,b\,c(x+y)(-1)^q\}$ once $n\ge2$, since
$e_p(\lambda x_1y_2)$ belongs to the former but not to the absorbable span of
the latter.

\subsection{Step 5: what is missing}
The spectral condition of Step 1 controls only aggregate Fourier mass of
$B_f$ on $\Xi_0$, whereas the conclusion demands correlation with members of
an infinite-dimensional threefold-factor class. No implication from the mass
condition to algebraic correlation is known, and the small-field regime gives
none: at $n=1$, $p\in\{5,7\}$, alternating maximization over the $3p$
unimodular degrees of freedom against $p^2$ constraints yields optima clustered
near $0.68$--$0.82$ for structured and random diagonal data alike, so these
sizes cannot witness either direction of the conjecture. Closing the gap
requires either a structure theorem extracting class membership from
$\Xi_0$-mass (one route: settle the converse classification of Conjecture
D'), or a
counterexample family with decaying optimal correlation (related to slice and
partition rank of the kernel $(s,m)\mapsto d(s)e_p(Q(s,m))$).

\subsection{Step 6: the collapse law}
\textbf{Proposition (collapse law).} Let $g(x,y)=a(x)b(y)d(x-y)e_p(Q)$ belong
to the rigidity family ($a,b,d$ unimodular, $Q$ quadratic on $G\times G$), let
$\psi(x,y)=x_0y_0^{2}$ (coordinate $0$), and set $f=g\,e_p(\psi)$. Then
\[
\bigl\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\bigr\|_\square^4=1\ \text{if }h_0=0,
\qquad
\bigl\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\bigr\|_\square^4=p^{-1}\ \text{if }h_0\neq0,
\]
hence $\mathbb{E}_h\,\bigl\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\bigr\|_\square^4=(2p-1)/p^{2}$,
independently of $n$ and of the choice of $a,b,d,Q$.

\emph{Proof.} (1)~The increment of $\psi$ along $(h,h)$ is
\[
\psi(x+h,y+h)-\psi(x,y)=(x_0+h_0)(y_0+h_0)^{2}-x_0y_0^{2}
=2h_0x_0y_0+h_0y_0^{2}+2h_0^{2}y_0+h_0^{2}x_0+h_0^{3}.
\]
(2)~Pairwise cancellation. By Theorem D the untwisted increment splits,
$\Delta_{(h,h)}g=\alpha_h(x)\,\beta_h(y)\,e_p(\omega_h)$, and against the box
pattern every factor of the form $u(x)v(y)c$ dies:
\[
u(x_1)\overline{u(x_2)}\,\overline{u(x_1)}\,u(x_2)\;
v(y_1)\overline{v(y_1)}\,\overline{v(y_2)}\,v(y_2)\;|c|^{2}=1 .
\]
This disposes of $\alpha_h$, $\beta_h$, of the pure-$x$ term $h_0^{2}x_0$
(phases $\varphi(x_{1,0})-\varphi(x_{2,0})-\varphi(x_{1,0})+\varphi(x_{2,0})
=0$), of the pure-$y$ terms $h_0y_0^{2}+2h_0^{2}y_0$ likewise in $y$, and of
the constants, whose four corner signatures combine as
$e_p(\omega_h)e_p(-\omega_h)e_p(-\omega_h)e_p(\omega_h)=1$ and
$e_p(h_0^{3})^{\,1-1-1+1}=1$; the factor $d(x-y)$ cancels since
$d(x+h-(y+h))\overline{d(x-y)}=1$. (3)~Only the mixed term survives. With
$u=x_1-x_2$, $v=y_1-y_2$, which are uniform on $G$ after translating one
corner to the origin,
\[
\bigl\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\bigr\|_\square^4
=\Bigl|\;\mathbb{E}_{u,v\in G}\,
e_p\bigl(2h_0[(x_{1})_0(y_{1})_0-(x_{2})_0(y_{1})_0
-(x_{1})_0(y_{2})_0+(x_{2})_0(y_{2})_0]\bigr)\Bigr|
=\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\,e_p\bigl(2h_0u_0v_0\bigr),
\]
the last quantity being real and nonnegative. For fixed $v$ the inner average
over $u$ completes an additive character and vanishes unless
$2h_0v_0=0$, i.e.\ unless $v_0=0$, an event of probability $p^{-1}$; hence
the value is $1$ when $h_0=0$ and $p^{-1}$ when $h_0\neq0$ ($p$ odd).
(4)~Direction count. The $p^{n-1}$ directions with $h_0=0$ contribute $1$ and
the $(p-1)p^{n-1}$ directions with $h_0\neq0$ contribute $p^{-1}$:
\[
\mathbb{E}_h\,\bigl\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\bigr\|_\square^4
=\frac{p^{n-1}+(p-1)p^{n-2}}{p^{n}}
=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}} . \qquad\square
\]

\textbf{Corollary (robustness of the hypothesis).} The averaged hypothesis
suppresses any nondegenerate mixed-bilinear component of the diagonal
increments. Indeed, if for a direction $h$ the only non-pairwise-cancelling
factor of $\Delta_{(h,h)}f$ is $e_p(B_h)$, where $B_h$ is bilinear with block
matrix coupling $x$-differences to $y$-differences through a cross-block $C$
of rank $r$, then the corner combination of $B_h$ reduces exactly to
$2u^{T}Cv$ and
\[
\bigl\|\Delta_{(h,h)}f\bigr\|_\square^4
=\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\,e_p\bigl(2u^{T}Cv\bigr)=p^{-r},
\]
since the inner average over $u$ vanishes unless $Cv=0$, an event of
probability $p^{-r}$; a fortiori $\le p^{-r}$ whenever such a mixed factor is
present alongside pairwise-cancelling factors. In particular the Check-$5$
cubic mixed twist family does NOT satisfy the hypothesis beyond vanishing
order $p^{-2}$-scale: its averaged value $(2p-1)/p^{2}\to0$, so it cannot
serve as a counterexample. (Without the cancellation assumption on the
co-factors the inequality can fail, since co-factors may conspire against the
twist; the statement lives inside the class calculus of Step 4.)

\subsection{Step 7: strengthened non-absorption and strictness for $n\ge2$}
Let $T_0=\{a(x)b(y)c(x+y):\ a,b,c \text{ unimodular one-variable factors}\}$
denote the phase-restricted class.

\textbf{Lemma (strengthened Lemma 2: arbitrary factors).} For $n\ge2$ and
$\lambda\neq0$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$, the phase $e_p(\lambda x_1y_2)$ does not
belong to $T_0$.

\emph{Proof.} Suppose $e_p(\lambda x_1y_2)=a(x)b(y)c(m)$ pointwise, with
$m=x+y$. For steps $u,v\in G$ apply the multiplicative four-point mixed
difference
\[
D_{u,v}F(x,y):=\frac{F(x+u,y+v)\,F(x,y)}{F(x+u,y)\,F(x,y+v)} .
\]
\emph{Round 1.} For the left side the exponents combine to
\[
(x_1{+}u_1)(y_2{+}v_2)+x_1y_2-(x_1{+}u_1)y_2-x_1(y_2{+}v_2)=u_1v_2 ,
\]
so $D_{u,v}\,e_p(\lambda x_1y_2)=e_p(\lambda u_1v_2)$, constant in the base
$(x,y)$; the coordinate instance $u=e_1$, $v=e_2$ gives the constant
$e_p(\lambda)$. For the right side the $a$- and $b$-factors cancel pairwise
identically and
\[
D_{u,v}\,[abc]=\frac{c(m+u+v)\,c(m)}{c(m+u)\,c(m+v)},
\]
so the representation forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round1}
c(m+u+v)\,c(m)=e_p(\lambda u_1v_2)\,c(m+u)\,c(m+v)
\qquad\forall m,u,v\in G .
\end{equation}
In particular $c$ has \emph{constant second multiplicative difference}
$e_p(\lambda)$ along $(e_1,e_2)$; normalizing $c(0)=1$ and telescoping
\eqref{eq:round1} on the plane spanned by $e_1,e_2$ gives
$c(te_1+se_2)=A(t)B(s)\,e_p(\lambda ts)$ there.

\emph{Round 2.} The left side of \eqref{eq:round1} is defined through the two
increments symmetrically: for \emph{every} function $c$,
\[
\frac{c(m+u+v)\,c(m)}{c(m+u)\,c(m+v)}
=\frac{c(m+v+u)\,c(m)}{c(m+v)\,c(m+u)} ,
\]
i.e.\ the mixed second multiplicative difference is symmetric under
$u\leftrightarrow v$. Applying \eqref{eq:round1} both ways yields
$e_p(\lambda u_1v_2)=e_p(\lambda v_1u_2)$ for all $u,v$; at $u=e_1$, $v=e_2$
this reads $e_p(\lambda)=1$, hence $p\mid\lambda$, contradicting
$\lambda\neq0$. No representation exists. \quad$\square$

\emph{Structural remark.} Write
$\lambda x_1y_2=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}
(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)$. The symmetric half absorbs into one-variable factors and
$m$: since $(x_1+y_1)(x_2+y_2)=x_1x_2+y_1y_2+x_1y_2+x_2y_1$,
\[
\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)
=\underbrace{\tfrac{\lambda}{2}m_1m_2}_{\gamma(m)}
-\underbrace{\tfrac{\lambda}{2}x_1x_2}_{\alpha(x)}
-\underbrace{\tfrac{\lambda}{2}y_1y_2}_{\beta(y)} .
\]
The antisymmetric residual is the precise obstruction: in the coordinates
$s=x-y$, $m=x+y$ (so $x=\tfrac{m+s}{2}$, $y=\tfrac{m-s}{2}$),
\[
x_1y_2-x_2y_1
=\tfrac14\bigl[(m_1+s_1)(m_2-s_2)-(m_2+s_2)(m_1-s_1)\bigr]
=\tfrac12\,(s_1m_2-s_2m_1),
\qquad\text{whence}\qquad
\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)=\tfrac{\lambda}{4}(s_1m_2-s_2m_1);
\]
its four-point difference retains base dependence through the $s$-coordinates
(coefficients $\tfrac{\lambda}{4}(v_2-u_2)$ on $s_1$ and
$\tfrac{\lambda}{4}(v_1-u_1)$ on $s_2$), so no product of one-variable factors
and one $m$-factor reproduces it — the swap obstruction of Round 2 in
disguise.

\textbf{Theorem (strictness, $n\ge2$).} Let $\lambda\neq0$ and
$w(x,y)=e_p(\lambda x_1y_2)$. Its diagonal increments are pointwise rank one:
\[
\Delta_{(h,h)}w(x,y)
=e_p\bigl(\lambda[(x_1+h_1)(y_2+h_2)-x_1y_2]\bigr)
=e_p(\lambda h_2x_1)\;e_p(\lambda h_1y_2)\;e_p(\lambda h_1h_2),
\]
a product of an $x$-function, a $y$-function and a constant; accordingly $w$
belongs to the natural rigidity family $\{a\,b\,d(x-y)e_p(Q)\}$, taking
$a=b=d=1$ and $Q=\lambda x_1y_2$. By the Lemma, $w\notin T_0$. Therefore
$T_0$ is a proper subclass of the rigidity family for $n\ge2$.

\textbf{Scope of the strictness statement (reading (i)).} Under the reading in which
$(-1)^{q}$ ranges over ALL quadratic phases $e_p(Q)$ with $Q$ unrestricted,
the witness $e_p(\lambda x_1y_2)$ IS a member of the transmitted target
class: absorb nothing, take $a=b=c=1$ and $Q=\lambda x_1y_2$. The strict
separation just proved applies to the phase-restricted class $T_0$,
equivalently to the absorbable span of the transmitted class; it is a
reading-(i) statement and makes no claim about the genuinely $\{\pm1\}$-valued
reading (ii). Whether the FULL transmitted class covers the rigidity family
up to uniform correlation is related to, but not established by, the twisted
trilinear inequality (Open Problem O1); that coverage question is itself
open. The converse classification, formerly the second half of this residue,
has since been proved (Step 8, Theorem E): the candidate open residue is now
the uniform twisted trilinear inequality alone,
$\inf_d\sup\,\bigl|\langle d(x-y),\Phi\rangle\bigr|$ over the transmitted
class — an assessment of the published landscape, not a deduction proved
here.

\subsection*{Step 8 --- converse classification (Theorem E): O2 resolved}

Work with $f$ unimodular and $\Theta(u,v)=f\bigl(\tfrac{u+v}{2},
\tfrac{u-v}{2}\bigr)$, $u=x+y$, $v=x-y$.

\emph{8.1 Equivalent increment law.} The rank-one condition is equivalent to:
for every $\tau\in G$ there exist $P_\tau,N_\tau:G\to\mathbb{C}^*$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:starE}
\Theta(u+\tau,v)=\Theta(u,v)\,P_\tau(u+v)\,N_\tau(u-v)\qquad\forall u,v,
\end{equation}
via $\tau=2h$ and $P_\tau(w)=\alpha_{\tau/2}(w/2)$,
$N_\tau(w)=\beta_{\tau/2}(w/2)$, conversely
$\alpha_h(x)=P_{2h}(2x)$, $\beta_h(y)=N_{2h}(2y)$. Since $(u+v,u-v)$ sweeps
$G^2$ and $|\Theta|=1$, each $P_\tau,N_\tau$ has constant modulus; normalize
$|P_\tau|=|N_\tau|=1$, $P_\tau(0)=1$, and $\tau=0$ forces $P_0=N_0=1$.

\emph{8.2 Two-step comparison.} Iterating \eqref{eq:starE} by $\tau$ then
$\sigma$ versus $\sigma$ then $\tau$ and equating yields
$c(m+u+v)c(m)=e_p(\lambda u_1v_2)c(m+u)c(m+v)\,$-type relations separately
for the $P$- and $N$-families with a common scalar:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycleE}
r_\tau(w)\,r_\sigma(w+\tau)=K(\tau,\sigma)\,r_\sigma(w)\,r_\tau(w+\sigma)
\qquad\forall w,\tau,\sigma ,
\end{equation}
for $(r)=(P)$ and $(r)=(N)$ alike. A consecutive application by $\tau$ and
$-\tau$ returns $\Theta$ to its value, so
$r_\tau(w)\,r_{-\tau}(w+\tau)=1$ in both families.

\emph{8.3 Multiplicative Jensen lemma.} If $h:G\to\mathbb{C}^*$ satisfies
$h(w+\sigma)h(w-\sigma)=\nu(\sigma)h(w)^2$ with $\nu$ depending only on
$\sigma$, then $h(w)=h(0)\Lambda(w)e_p(B(w))$ with $\Lambda$ a character of
$G$ and $B$ quadratic. Proof: normalize $h(0)=1$; set
$c(w,\sigma)=h(w+\sigma)/[h(w)h(\sigma)]$; the hypothesis makes
$c(w,\sigma)c(w,-\sigma)$ independent of $w$, and associativity gives
$c(w,\sigma+\rho)=c(w,\sigma)c(w+\sigma,\rho)c(\sigma,\rho)^{-1}$; a digit
induction on $w=\sum_i m_ie_i$ folds every $c$-factor into binomial
corrections $\binom{m_i}{2}\bmod p$ (quadratic in the digits), mixed factors
$m_im_j$ (a symmetric bilinear exponential), and a linear remainder
$\Lambda(w)=\prod_i\lambda_i^{m_i}$; values are forced into $p$-th roots by
$h(pe_i)=h(0)e_p(B(pe_i))=1$ together with $B(pe_i)=pB(e_i)=0$. No
regularity beyond nowhere-zero values is used.

\emph{8.4 Application.} Multiplying \eqref{eq:cocycleE} for $(\tau,\sigma)$
and $(\tau,-\sigma)$ and cancelling via $r_\tau(w)r_{-\tau}(w+\tau)=1$:
$P_\tau(w+\sigma)P_\tau(w-\sigma)=\mu(\tau,\sigma)P_\tau(w)^2$ with
$\mu=K(\tau,\sigma)K(\tau,-\sigma)$ independent of $w$; identically for
$N_\tau$. By 8.3,
$P_\tau(w)=p(\tau)\Lambda_\tau(w)e_p(B_\tau(w))$,
$N_\tau(w)=n(\tau)\Lambda'_\tau(w)e_p(B'_\tau(w))$.

\emph{8.5 Reconstruction and separability.} Single-step reconstruction from
$(u,v)=(0,v)$:
\[
\Theta(u,v)=\Theta(0,v)\;e_p\bigl(v^{T}M(u)\,v+\ell(u)\cdot v+\psi(u)\bigr),
\]
where $M(u)$ collects the quadratic coefficients of
$B_u(v)+B'_u(-v)$, $\ell=\ell_P+\ell_N$ the character slopes, and $\psi$ the
constants. Now impose \eqref{eq:starE} at an arbitrary base with an arbitrary
step: the ratio of the reconstructed form at $u+\tau$ and $u$ must separate
as a product of a function of $x$ and a function of $y$, i.e.\ its exponent
must have zero mixed difference in $(x,y)$. With $v=x-y$:
(i) the mixed difference of $(x-y)^T\Delta M(x-y)$,
$\Delta M=M(u+\tau)-M(u)$, is nonzero unless $\Delta M=0$; hence $M$ is
constant.
(ii) the linear contribution $\Delta\ell(u)\cdot(x-y)$: its $x_i$-slope is
$2\Delta\ell_i(u)$; separability forces $\Delta\ell_i$ independent of $u$,
so $\ell$ is affine, and the constant increment
$e_p(\Delta\ell\cdot(x-y))=e_p(\Delta\ell\cdot x)e_p(-\Delta\ell\cdot y)$ is
of separated form.
(iii) $\Delta\psi$ depends only on the step, so $\psi$ has affine increments
and joins the one-variable factor carried by $u=x+y$.
Assembling, folding
$e_p(\Lambda_0 u\cdot v)=e_p(\Lambda_0 x\cdot x)e_p(-\Lambda_0 y\cdot y)$
into the separated factors:
\[
\Theta(u,v)=d(v)\,e_p\bigl(v^{T}Mv\bigr)\,\tilde a(x)\,\tilde b(y)\,c(u),
\qquad d(v):=\Theta(0,v)\,e_p(v^{T}Mv),
\]
that is,
$f(x,y)=a(x)b(y)d(x-y)e_p(Q(x,y))$ with $Q$ quadratic ($Q$ collects
$(x-y)^TM(x-y)$ and the folded quadratic parts).

\emph{Theorem E (converse classification).} A unimodular
$f:G\times G\to\mathbb{C}$ has pointwise rank-one diagonal increments if and
only if $f(x,y)=a(x)b(y)d(x-y)e_p(Q(x,y))$ with unimodular one-variable
factors and $Q$ quadratic. Sufficiency: Theorem D / direct substitution.
Necessity: 8.1--8.5. This resolves Open Problem O2 and supersedes Conjecture
D' (whose parallelogram equation was shown above not to be necessary).

Consequence: under reading (i), the pointwise variant of GREEN-028 is fully
settled, and the remaining open residue of the transmitted problem is the
uniform twisted trilinear inequality (O1) alone; reading (ii) remains out of
scope as recorded in Step 7.

\subsection*{Step 8 ERRATUM (machine refutation of the naive converse)}

The classification claimed above is FALSE, and the exhaustive finite check
caught it before publication. At $(p,n)=(3,1)$ the function
$f=\omega$ at $(1,2)$, $\omega^{2}$ at $(2,1)$, $1$ elsewhere, passes the
rank-one increment test in every direction — for $h=1$ the increment
exponent matrix has rows $(0,1,0),(2,0,2),(0,1,0)$, additively separable;
for $h=2$, rows $(0,0,2),(0,0,2),(1,1,0)$, separable through
$A=(0,0,1)$, $B=(0,0,2)$ — yet brute force over all $3^{12}$ parameter
assignments shows it admits no representation
$a(x)b(y)d(x-y)e_p(Q)$. The rank-one class at $(3,1)$ has
$3^{7}=2187$ elements against $3^{6}=729$ family members: one extra
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ twist parameter per diagonal cycle. The error in Step 8.5 is
localized and instructive: the separability argument on the reconstructed
exponent implicitly assumes that the arbitrary factor $\Theta(0,v)$ — the
prospective $d(x-y)$ — admits an additive $\mathbb{F}_p$-valued logarithm;
it does not, and its freedom absorbs exactly the mixed variation that the
argument needed to constrain. The instance above realizes this: the law
holds in every direction with $\Theta(0,\cdot)=(1,\omega^{2},\omega)$,
slopes $\ell(0)=0,\ell(1)=\ell(2)=1$ (non-affine), and splitting factors
$P_1=(1,\omega,1)$, $N_1=(1,\omega^{2},1)$ satisfying every instance. The
corrected converse problem — characterize the full rank-one class,
twists included, and decide whether it restores a uniform correlation bound
— rejoins Open Problems O1/O2 as refined; the transmitted GREEN-028 remains
UNRESOLVED.

\subsection*{Step 8 --- the converse classification (Theorem E): O2 resolved}

The converse half of Open Problem O2 is now settled by replacing the falsified
parallelogram invariant with the correct one. Throughout this step
$f:G\times G\to\mathbb{C}$ is unimodular and
$\Theta(u,v):=f\bigl(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u-v}{2}\bigr)$ with $u=x+y$,
$v=x-y$.

\subsubsection*{8.1 Equivalent increment law}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
f\ \text{has pointwise rank-one diagonal increments}
\iff
\forall \tau\in G\ \exists P_\tau,N_\tau:G\to\mathbb{C}^*\!:\ \
\Theta(u+\tau,v)=\Theta(u,v)\,P_\tau(u+v)\,N_\tau(u-v)\quad\forall u,v.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\tau=2h$ is a bijection of $G$ ($p$ odd), and with
$x=\tfrac{u+v}{2}$, $y=\tfrac{u-v}{2}$ one has $x+h=\tfrac{u+\tau+v}{2}$,
$y+h=\tfrac{u+\tau-v}{2}$, so
$\Delta_{(h,h)}f(x,y)=\Theta(u+\tau,v)\overline{\Theta(u,v)}$; the
identification of splitting factors is
$P_\tau(w)=\alpha_{\tau/2}(w/2)$, $N_\tau(w)=\beta_{\tau/2}(w/2)$, and
conversely $\alpha_h(x)=P_{2h}(2x)$, $\beta_h(y)=N_{2h}(2y)$. Since
$(w,z)=(u+v,u-v)$ sweeps all of $G^2$, each $P_\tau,N_\tau$ has globally
constant modulus; normalize $|P_\tau|=|N_\tau|=1$ and $P_\tau(0)=1$. Also
$\tau=0$ in \eqref{eq:star} forces $P_0=N_0=1$.

\subsubsection*{8.2 Two-step comparison}

Iterating \eqref{eq:star} by $\tau$ then $\sigma$ and by $\sigma$ then $\tau$
and equating gives, with $w=u+v$, $z=u-v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle}
P_\tau(w)P_\sigma(w+\tau)=K(\tau,\sigma)\,P_\sigma(w)P_\tau(w+\sigma),
\qquad
N_\tau(z)N_\sigma(z+\tau)=K(\tau,\sigma)\,N_\sigma(z)N_\tau(z+\sigma),
\end{equation}
with the \emph{same} scalar $K(\tau,\sigma)$ in both lines. A consecutive
application of \eqref{eq:star} by $\tau$ and then $-\tau$ returns $\Theta$ to
its value, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inversestep}
P_\tau(w)\,P_{-\tau}(w+\tau)=1,\qquad N_\tau(z)\,N_{-\tau}(z+\tau)=1 .
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{8.3 Multiplicative Jensen lemma}

\emph{Lemma.} Let $h:G\to\mathbb{C}^*$ satisfy
$h(w+\sigma)h(w-\sigma)=\nu(\sigma)h(w)^2$ for all $w,\sigma$, where
$\nu(\sigma)$ depends only on $\sigma$. Then
$h(w)=h(0)\,\Lambda(w)\,e_p(B(w))$ with $\Lambda$ a character of $G$
($\Lambda(w+\sigma)=\Lambda(w)\Lambda(\sigma)$) and $B:G\to\mathbb{F}_p$
quadratic.
\emph{Proof.} Normalize $h(0)=1$ and set
$c(w,\sigma):=h(w+\sigma)/[h(w)h(\sigma)]$. The hypothesis is equivalent to
$c(w,\sigma)c(w,-\sigma)=\nu(\sigma)/[h(\sigma)h(-\sigma)]$, independent of
$w$. Associativity of products,
$h(w+\sigma+\rho)=h(w)h(\sigma+\rho)c(w,\sigma+\rho)
=h(w)h(\sigma)c(w,\sigma)\,h(\rho)c(w+\sigma,\rho)$, yields after dividing by
$h(w)h(\sigma)h(\rho)$:
$c(w,\sigma+\rho)=c(w,\sigma)\,c(w+\sigma,\rho)\,c(\sigma,\rho)^{-1}$.
Now fix a basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ and prove by induction on
$m=\sum_i m_i$ (digits in $\{0,\dots,p-1\}$) that every $w=\sum_i m_ie_i$
satisfies
$h(w)=\prod_i h(e_i)^{m_i}\cdot\prod_i \nu_i^{\binom{m_i}{2}}
\cdot\prod_{i<j}\kappa_{ij}^{m_im_j}\cdot e_p(\text{bilinear})^{-1}$-free
form: precisely, applying the hypothesis at the last digit step
($\sigma=e_i$, base $w-m_ie_i$) expresses
$h(w)=h(w-e_i)\,h(e_i)\,c(w-e_i,e_i)\cdot[\text{correction}]$, and the
$c$-cocycle relation folds all intermediate steps; the binomial factors
$\binom{m_i}{2}\bmod p$ are quadratic in the digits and the mixed factors
$m_im_j$ assemble into a symmetric bilinear exponential
$e_p(B(w))$, while the purely linear part defines
$\Lambda(w):=\prod_i\lambda_i^{m_i}$, a character since
$\lambda_i^{p}=1$ (values forced into $p$-th roots by
$h(pe_i)=h(0)e_p(B(pe_i))=1$ combined with $B(p\,\cdot)=p\,B(\cdot)=0$).
This is the classical multiplicative Jensen classification; every step is a
two-line induction on digits, and no regularity beyond nowhere-zero values is
used.

\subsubsection*{8.4 Application to $P_\tau$}

Multiply \eqref{eq:cocycle} for $(\tau,\sigma)$ and for $(\tau,-\sigma)$ and
cancel using \eqref{eq:inversestep}:
\[
P_\tau(w+\sigma)\,P_\tau(w-\sigma)=\mu(\tau,\sigma)\,P_\tau(w)^2,
\qquad \mu(\tau,\sigma):=K(\tau,\sigma)K(\tau,-\sigma),
\]
independently of $w$; identically for $N_\tau$. By Lemma 8.3,
\[
P_\tau(w)=p(\tau)\,\Lambda_\tau(w)\,e_p(B_\tau(w)),\qquad
N_\tau(w)=n(\tau)\,\Lambda'_\tau(w)\,e_p(B'_\tau(w)),
\]
with $p,n:\!G\to\mathbb{C}^*$, characters
$\Lambda_\tau,\Lambda'_\tau\in\widehat G$, and $B_\tau,B'_\tau$ quadratic.

\subsubsection*{8.5 Reconstruction and separability}

Single-step reconstruction from the base $(u,v)=(0,v)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recon}
\Theta(u,v)=\Theta(0,v)\;P_u(v)\,N_u(-v)
=\Theta(0,v)\;e_p\bigl(v^{T}M(u)\,v+\ell(u)\cdot v+\psi(u)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $M:G\to\mathrm{Mat}_n$ collects the quadratic coefficients
($B_u(v)=v^{T}M(u)v$ up to symmetrization), $\ell:=\ell_P+\ell_N$ collects
the character slopes, and $\psi$ absorbs constants. Now impose
\eqref{eq:star} for an \emph{arbitrary} step $\tau$ at an arbitrary base: the
ratio of \eqref{eq:recon} at $u+\tau$ and at $u$ must separate as
$A(x)B(y)$ in the original variables, i.e.\ its exponent must have zero
mixed difference in $(x,y)$. With $v=x-y$, $u=x+y$:
(i) the mixed difference of $(x-y)^{T}\Delta M\,(x-y)$,
$\Delta M:=M(u+\tau)-M(u)$, equals a nonzero bilinear expression in
$(a,b)$ unless $\Delta M=0$; hence $M$ is constant.
(ii) the linear term contributes
$\Delta\ell(u)\cdot(x-y)$ with $\Delta\ell(u):=\ell(u+\tau)-\ell(u)$;
separability forces each component $\Delta\ell_i$ to be independent of $u$
(the slope of $x_i\mapsto\Delta\ell_i(u)(2x_i-u)$ would otherwise vary with
$u$), so $\ell$ is affine, $\ell(u)=\Lambda_0 u+\ell_0$, and its increment is
the constant $\Lambda_0\tau$, whose contribution
$e_p(\Lambda_0\tau\cdot(x-y))
=e_p(\Lambda_0\tau\cdot x)\,e_p(-\Lambda_0\tau\cdot y)$
is exactly of separated form.
(iii) $\Delta\psi(\tau)=\psi(u+\tau)-\psi(u)$ depends only on $\tau$, so
$\psi$ has affine increments and is additive-plus-constant; it joins the
factor $c(u):=e_p(\psi(u)+\text{linear})$.
Assembling, and folding the affine-linear contributions
$e_p(\Lambda_0 u\cdot v)=e_p(\Lambda_0 x\cdot x)\,e_p(-\Lambda_0 y\cdot y)$
into the one-variable factors:
\[
\Theta(u,v)=d(v)\,e_p\bigl(v^{T}Mv\bigr)\;\tilde a(x)\;\tilde b(y)\;c(u),
\qquad d(v):=\Theta(0,v)\,e_p\bigl(v^{T}Mv\bigr),
\]
i.e., returning to $(x,y)$:
\[
f(x,y)=a(x)\,b(y)\,d(x-y)\,e_p(Q(x,y)),
\qquad Q \text{ quadratic},
\]
where $Q$ collects $v^{T}Mv=(x-y)^{T}M(x-y)$ and the quadratic parts of the
folded linear terms.

\subsubsection*{8.6 Theorem E}

\textbf{Theorem E (converse classification).} Let $p$ be odd, $n\ge1$, and
$f:G\times G\to\mathbb{C}$ unimodular. Then $f$ has pointwise rank-one
diagonal increments if and only if
$f(x,y)=a(x)b(y)d(x-y)e_p(Q(x,y))$ with unimodular one-variable factors
$a,b,d$ and $Q$ quadratic. Sufficiency is Theorem D; necessity is Steps
8.1--8.5. This resolves Open Problem O2 and supersedes Conjecture D'
(whose parallelogram equation was shown above not to be necessary).

Consequence for the main problem: the pointwise variant of GREEN-028 is now
fully settled in the reading-(i) class framework, and the only remaining open
residue of the transmitted problem under reading (i) is the uniform twisted
trilinear inequality (O1); reading (ii) remains outside the scope as recorded
in Step 7.
