\begin{abstract}
Let $\A$ be the smallest set of positive integers containing $2$ and $3$ and
closed under the operation $(a_1,a_2)\mapsto a_1a_2-1$. Green's problem
GREEN-036 asks whether $\A$ has positive asymptotic density.
\textbf{The problem remains open.} This dossier contributes: (i) complete
proofs of a well-foundedness structure theorem, of the modular obstruction
(Lemma~A: $\A$ avoids $1 \bmod 3$, whence upper density $\bar d(\A)\le 2/3$),
and of an explicit $\lfloor(\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor-1)^2/4\rfloor$
$\sim 0.5203\,(\ln N)^2$ lower bound (Lemma~B); (ii) an exact, independently
cross-checked census $\F(10^8)=|\A\cap[1,10^8]|=55{,}939{,}931$ with per-decade
statistics showing monotonically rising density
$F(10^k)/10^k = 0.500\to0.559$ and local power-law exponents descending to
$1.0079$; (iii) a mean-field self-consistency heuristic
$\sum_{b\in\A} b^{-(1+\alpha)}=1$, which predicts $\alpha^\ast\approx0.4734$
and is \emph{quantitatively rejected} by the census (factor $9124$), indicating
that membership correlations, not pseudorandomness, govern $\A$; and (iv) a
residue-class census showing that among all moduli $m\le500$, only $m=3$
yields a proper closed residue set, so no improvement on the bound $2/3$ is
known. All computational claims are exactly verified by the accompanying
scripts; proved, computed, and heuristic statements are labeled throughout.
An attack iteration additionally proves two obstructions --- an affine-image
overlap collapse $S(B)=\frac{1}{\pi^2}(\log B)^3+O((\log B)^2)$ and a
multiplication-table cap (Erd\H{o}s/Ford) ruling out one-step bootstraps ---
plus complement rigidity along primes and the exact powers-of-two membership
pattern up to $2^{40}$; the problem remains open, with the decisive gap now
isolated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem statement and status}\label{sec:problem}

Following \cite{green100}, let $\A\subseteq\Z_{>0}$ be minimal with
\[
2,3\in\A,\qquad a_1,a_2\in\A \;\Longrightarrow\; a_1a_2-1\in\A,
\]
and ask whether the asymptotic density
$d(\A)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\F(N)/N$, $\F(N):=|\A\cap[1,N]|$,
exists and is positive. The question is \textbf{open}. This manuscript does
not settle it; it establishes partial results (\S\S~\ref{sec:structure}--%
\ref{sec:residues}), an exact computational census (\S\ref{sec:census}), and a
heuristic analysis including its own empirical rejection (\S\ref{sec:heuristic}).

\paragraph{Status labels.} Throughout, \textbf{[PROVEN]} marks statements with
complete proofs below; \textbf{[COMPUTED]} marks exact finite computations
performed by the verification script with all checks passing;
\textbf{[HEURISTIC]} marks formal calculations whose hypotheses are not
verified; conclusions that remain unknown are marked \textbf{[OPEN]}.

\section{Well-foundedness and an exact characterization}\label{sec:structure}

\begin{lemma}[Structural fact]\label{lem:struct}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
One has $1\notin\A$, and if $n=a_1a_2-1\in\A$ with $a_1,a_2\in\A$ then
$a_1,a_2\ge 2$ and
\[
a_1,\;a_2\ \le\ \frac{n+1}{2}\ <\ n .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Seeds are $2,3$ and $ab-1\ge 2\cdot2-1=3$ for $a,b\ge2$, so $1\notin\A$ and
every factor lies in $\Z_{\ge2}$. Then $a_1a_2=n+1$ and each factor is at most
$(n+1)/2$, which is $<n$ for $n>1$.
\end{proof}

Let $T$ denote the class of generation trees: finite binary trees whose leaves
are labeled $2$ or $3$ and whose internal node of value $n$ has children with
values $a_1,a_2$ satisfying $n=a_1a_2-1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:struct} shows values
strictly decrease along edges toward leaves, so every element of $\A$ has at
least one such tree.

\begin{theorem}[Exact characterization]\label{thm:char}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
\[
n\in\A\iff n\in\{2,3\}\ \text{or}\ \exists\,a,b\in\A:\ n=ab-1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) Immediate for generators; otherwise take the root split of a
generation tree for $n$. ($\Leftarrow$) Let $A'=\{2,3\}\cup\{ab-1: a,b\in\A\}$.
Since every $n\in\A\setminus\{2,3\}$ equals $a_1a_2-1$ with strictly smaller
$a_1,a_2\in\A$, induction on $\max(a_1,a_2)$ (well-founded by
Lemma~\ref{lem:struct}) gives $\A\subseteq A'$; conversely closure gives
$A'\subseteq\A$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:char} says $\A$ is the least fixed point of the operator
$S\mapsto S\cup\{ab-1\}$, justifying both the fixpoint cross-check and the
increasing-order dynamic program of \S\ref{sec:algo}.

\section{Exact generation algorithm and correctness}\label{sec:algo}

The verification script computes $\A\cap[1,X]$ as follows: a bytearray records
membership; a min-heap holds discovered-but-unexpanded values; a sorted list
\texttt{small} holds $\A\cap[1,\sqrt X]$.

For each popped minimum $m$ (pops occur in increasing order because inserted
values $n=ma-1\ge 2m-1>m$ always exceed the current pop), expand partners
$a\le\min(m,\lfloor X/m\rfloor)\le\sqrt X$ in ascending order and insert new
values $ma-1$.

\begin{theorem}[Exactness]\label{thm:exact}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
At termination the marked bytes are exactly $\A\cap[1,X]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Soundness}: every inserted value is $ma-1$ with $m,a$ already known to
lie in $\A$, hence belongs to $\A$. \emph{Completeness}: we show by strong
induction on $n$ the claim $C(n)$: before any pop of value $>n$, all elements
of $\A\cap[1,n]$ are discovered. $C(1)$ is trivial. Assume $C(n-1)$ and let
$n'\in\A$, $n'\le n$. If $n'$ is a seed it was inserted initially. Otherwise
$n'=ab-1$ with $a,b\in\A$ and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:struct},
$\max(a,b)\le(n'+1)/2<n$. Let $M=\max(a,b)$. By $C(M)$ (induction, since
$M\le n-1$) $M$ is discovered before pops exceed $M$; since pops increase, $M$
is then popped and expanded while the heap minimum is still $\le M<n$. At that
moment the partner $\min(a,b)\le M$ is already discovered (induction), so
$n'=Mb'-1$ with $b'=\min(a,b)$ is inserted during $M$'s expansion. Hence all of
$\A\cap[1,X]$ is discovered before the first pop exceeding $X$, and each of its
elements is popped and expanded before termination. Finally every pair
$(a,b)$, $ab-1\le X$, is consumed when its larger factor is expanded, because
the smaller factor is $\le\min(M,\lfloor X/M\rfloor)\le\sqrt X$ and therefore
present in \texttt{small}; so no products are missed even though expansion only
scans $\A\cap[1,\sqrt X]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\textbf{[COMPUTED]}
As an independent check, naive fixpoint iteration
$S\leftarrow S\cup\{ab-1\le X\}$ from $S=\{2,3\}$ reproduces the DP census
exactly on $[1,3000]$ ($|\A|=1358$ on both sides).
\end{remark}

Complexity is near-linear in practice: expansion work is
$\sum_{m\in\A}\bigl|\A\cap[1,\min(m,X/m)]\bigr|$, dominated by
$X\sum_{t\le\sqrt X}\F(t)/t^2$ after the substitution $t=X/m$. The production
run at $X=10^8$ took $6$ min $31$ s in pure Python.

\section{Lemma A: the modular obstruction and the \texorpdfstring{$2/3$}{2/3} ceiling}\label{sec:lemmaA}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma A]\label{lem:A}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
No element of $\A$ is congruent to $1\pmod 3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in $\Z/3$. The seed residues are $2$ and $0$, both in $S=\{0,2\}$. For
$r,s\in S$ the value $rs-1\pmod3$: if $r=0$ then $rs-1\equiv-1\equiv2$; if
$s=0$ likewise $rs-1\equiv2$; and $r=s=2$ gives $2\cdot2-1\equiv4-1\equiv0$.
Thus $rs-1\in S$ whenever $r,s\in S$: $S$ is closed under the operation and
contains the seed residues, so by minimality of $\A$ its residues lie in $S$;
equivalently no element of $\A$ is congruent to $1\pmod3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:density}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
The upper asymptotic density satisfies $\bar d(\A)\le 2/3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each of the two admissible classes modulo $3$ carries at most $N/3+O(1)$
elements below $N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\textbf{[COMPUTED]}
All $55{,}939{,}931$ census elements below $10^8$ avoid $1\bmod3$: zero
violations, consistent with Lemma~A.
\end{remark}

Note that $\A$ is a \emph{proper} subset of the allowed classes: e.g.\
$6,11,62\notin\A$ although none is $1\bmod3$ (their $n+1$ admit no divisor pair
from $\A$).

\section{Residue sets modulo arbitrary moduli}\label{sec:residues}

For a modulus $m$ let $S_m$ be the minimal subset of $\Z/m$ containing
$\{2\bmod m,\,3\bmod m\}$ closed under $(r,s)\mapsto rs-1$, computable by
fixpoint iteration.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:S_m}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
$\{\,a\bmod m : a\in\A\,\}=S_m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
($\subseteq$) By induction on generation trees: reduction mod $m$ commutes with
the operation, and $S_m$ contains the seed residues and is closed, so all node
labels reduce into $S_m$. ($\supseteq$) Each element of $S_m$ arises after
finitely many closure steps from the seed residues; induction on the step count
lifts it, step by step, through the operation back to actual elements of $\A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:crt}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
$\bar d(\A)\le |S_m|/m$ for every $m$; if $\gcd(m_i,m_j)=1$ then constraints
combine, $\bar d(\A)\le\prod_i |S_{m_i}|/m_i$ (CRT).
\end{corollary}

\end{remark}

\section{Lemma B: an explicit logarithmic lower bound}\label{sec:lemmaB}

Two one-parameter families live in $\A$ \textbf{[PROVEN]}:
\begin{align}
P_k &= 2^k+1 && (k\ge1), && \text{orbit of }3\text{ under }x\mapsto 2x-1,\\
V_j &= \tfrac{3^{j+1}+1}{2} && (j\ge1), && \text{orbit of }2\text{ under }x\mapsto 3x-1 ,
\end{align}
the latter solving $t_k-\tfrac12=3^k(t_0-\tfrac12)$. Closure then produces all
pairwise images.

\begin{lemma}[Lemma B]\label{lem:B}\textbf{[PROVEN]}
For all $N\ge8$,
\[
\F(N)\ \ge\ \Bigl\lfloor\tfrac{(\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor-1)^2}{4}\Bigr\rfloor
\ \ge\ \tfrac{(\log_2 N-2)^2}{4}
\ =\ \frac{(\ln N)^2}{4\ln^2 2}+O(\ln N),
\]
with explicit constant $c=1/(4\ln^2 2)=0.5203\ldots$
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define, for integers $0\le i\le j$,
\[
z_{ij}:=\bigl(2^{i+1}+1\bigr)\bigl(2^{j+1}+1\bigr)-1 \in \A ,
\]
so that $z_{ij}=2^{i+j+2}+2^{i+1}+2^{j+1}$.

\emph{Distinctness.} Consider cases.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $i<j$: $z_{ij}=2^{i+1}\bigl(1+2^{j-i}+2^{j+1}\bigr)$ with odd bracket,
hence $\vtwo(z_{ij})=i+1$ and odd part $q=1+2^{j-i}+2^{j+1}$.
\item $i=j\ge1$: $z_{ii}=2^{i+2}(2^i+1)$, so $\vtwo(z_{ii})=i+2$ and odd part
$2^i+1$.
\item $i=j=0$: $z_{00}=8=2^3$, the only pure power of two in the family (every
off-diagonal entry has odd part $q\ge1+2+8>1$ and value $\ge14$; every diagonal
entry with $i\ge1$ has odd part $2^i+1>1$ and value $\ge24$).
\end{enumerate}
Suppose $z_{ij}=z_{i'j'}$. If both pairs are off-diagonal, equal $\vtwo$ gives
$i=i'$, and equality of odd parts reads
$2^{\,j-i}\bigl(1+2^{i+1}\bigr)=2^{\,j'-i}\bigl(1+2^{i+1}\bigr)$; cancelling the
odd integer $1+2^{i+1}$ forces $j=j'$. If both are diagonal, equal $\vtwo$
forces $i=i'$. An off-diagonal/diagonal collision would force simultaneously
$i+1=i'+2$ and $1+2^{d}+2^{d+i+1}=2^{i'}+1$ for some $d\ge1$, i.e.\
$2^{d}\bigl(1+2^{i+1}\bigr)=2^{i'}$: impossible, since a pure power of two
cannot be divisible by the odd integer $1+2^{i+1}\ge3$. Hence
$(i,j)\mapsto z_{ij}$ is injective on $\{0\le i\le j\}$.

\emph{Counting.} Let $\lambda=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$ and suppose
$s:=i+j+3\le\lambda$. Then
\[
z_{ij}=2^{s-1}+2^{i+1}+2^{j+1}
\le 2^{s-1}+2^{s-2}+2^{s-2}=2^s,
\]
with strict inequality (equality would require $i+1=j+1=s-2$, i.e.\
$s=2i+3=2(s-2)+3$, absurd). So $z_{ij}<2^s\le N$. All pairs with
$0\le i\le j$, $i+j\le\lambda-3$ therefore give distinct members of
$\A\cap[1,N]$, and they number
\[
\sum_{s=0}^{\lambda-3}\bigl(\lfloor s/2\rfloor+1\bigr)
=\Bigl\lfloor\tfrac{(\lambda-1)^2}{4}\Bigr\rfloor . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Mixed family]\textbf{[COMPUTED; injectivity unproven]}\label{rem:mixed}
Set $U_i=2^{i+1}+1$, $V_j=(3^{j+1}+1)/2$, and $w_{ij}=U_iV_j-1\in\A$. A full
rectangle of parameters would yield constant
$(\ln N)^2/(2\ln2\ln3)=0.6566\ldots(\ln N)^2$ if $(i,j)\mapsto w_{ij}$ were
injective; the $2$-adic cascade recovers $\min(i+1,\vtwo(V_j-1))$ but ties block
a complete proof. Computationally, up to $10^8$ all $216$ generated values are
distinct and lie in $\A$ (zero collisions); likewise all $168$ values $z_{ij}$
of Lemma~B lie in $\A$ against the proven floor $156$ at $N=10^8$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scale honesty]
At $N=10^8$ these bounds produce $168$ elements against the true
$\F(10^8)=55{,}939{,}931$: exponentially far from observed behavior. Their
value is unconditional methodology, not approximation.
\end{remark}

\section{Mean-field heuristic: derivation and empirical rejection}\label{sec:heuristic}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:char}, membership is the OR over divisor pairs:
$n\in\A$ iff $n\in\{2,3\}$ or $n+1=ab$ with $a,b\in\A$. Suppose

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(H1)} \emph{equidistribution:} for each $b$,
$|\A\cap[1,M]\cap(-1\bmod b)|\approx\F(M)/b$ uniformly in $b$.

\medskip
Counting candidate images $ba-1\le N$ over all $b\in\A$ then gives
\begin{equation}
\F(N)\ \approx\ \sum_{b\in\A,\ b\le (N+1)/3}\frac{\F(N/b)}{b}. \tag{$\dagger$}
\end{equation}
Inserting the ansatz $\F(N)\sim cN^{\alpha}$ into $(\dagger)$ and cancelling
$cN^{\alpha}$ yields the \emph{self-consistency equation}
\begin{equation}
\sum_{b\in\A} b^{-(1+\alpha)}=1. \tag{$\ast$}
\end{equation}
Because the truncated harmonic sum diverges like $\hat\delta\ln X$
(computed: $H_X=9.1036$ at $X=10^8$, increments $\to\approx1.287$/decade), the
left side of $(\ast)$ decreases continuously from $\infty$ to $0$ on
$\alpha\in(0,\infty)$, so \emph{if} (H1) and the ansatz hold there is a unique
$\alpha^\ast>0$ \textbf{[HEURISTIC]}.

\paragraph{Numerics [COMPUTED].} Exact truncated sums below $2\cdot10^5$, and
$64$ logarithmic buckets per decade above (midpoint error negligible relative
to reported digits), give
\[
\alpha^\ast_{\mathrm{trunc}}(10^8)=0.473442
\quad(\text{residual }+2.5\cdot10^{-11}),
\]
already stable at $10^6$ ($0.472709$). The integral surrogate
$\delta\int_2^\infty t^{-1-\alpha}dt=\delta\,2^{-\alpha}/\alpha$ gives
$\alpha^\ast(0.5594)=0.4185$ and $\alpha^\ast(2/3)=0.4785$.

\paragraph{Rejection [COMPUTED vs.\ HEURISTIC].} The mean field predicts
$\F(10^8)\approx(10^8)^{0.4734}\approx6131$ against the exact
$55{,}939{,}931$: off by a factor $9124$. Simultaneously the direct fit gives
$\hat\alpha\approx1.016>1>\alpha^\ast$. The model is quantitatively wrong, and
its failure mechanism is identifiable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Self-affine imaging.} Each $b\in\A$ transports an entire
near-copy $\{ba-1: a\in\A\}$ into $\A$; conditional on $b$, membership of
$ba-1$ mirrors membership of $a$ rather than behaving like an independent
draw. The OR-condition over divisor pairs creates positive correlations that
inflate density beyond products of marginals.
\item \emph{Modular rigidity.} Equidistribution fails absolutely on class
$1\bmod3$ (empty intersection with $\A$), and Proposition~\ref{prop:S_m}
formalizes such constraints for every modulus.
\end{enumerate}
Equation $(\ast)$ therefore describes a random model of $\A$, not $\A$ itself.
Its interest is negative but sharp: GREEN-036 is not governed by
pseudorandom multiplicative statistics, and any successful approach must
exploit the correlated, self-affine structure.

\section{Outlook: what is missing}\label{sec:outlook}

\textbf{[OPEN]} To prove $d(\A)>0$ one would want a lower bound $\F(N)\ge cN$,
plausibly via eventual containment of an arithmetic progression of allowed
class, or a bootstrapping lemma converting lower density at height $T$ into
higher density at height $T'>T$. Obstructions are visible at small height:
$6,11,62\notin\A$ and the gap $2159$--$2169$ show $\A$ misses allowed classes
early, so any progression claim requires a threshold phenomenon. To prove
$d(\A)=0$ one must reverse a trend that rises across eight decades of exact
computation; no mechanism suggests itself. Between the two, the only proven
ceiling is $2/3$ (Lemma~A), the residue method is exhausted for $m\le500$, and
the proven growth floor is $(\ln N)^2/(4\ln^22)$. Finding any genuinely new
constraint---larger-modulus rigidity, $p$-adic restrictions, or complement
stability---would constitute real progress on GREEN-036.

The attack iteration of Section~\ref{sec:attack} adds two rigorous
obstructions (image-overlap collapse; multiplication-table cap), rigidity
lemmas for complements along primes, and an exact computed membership pattern
for powers of two; it also isolates the single open statement (the GAP below)
that separates the current technology from a resolution.

\section{Attack iteration: obstructions and rigidity}\label{sec:attack}

Throughout, $T_b(x)=bx-1$ for $b\in\A$.

\subsection{L1: image counting cap}

\begin{proposition}[exact image sizes and overlaps]\label{prop:L1}
For $b\ge2$: $|T_b(\A)\cap[1,N]|=\F(\lfloor (N+1)/b\rfloor)$ exactly, and for
$b_1\ne b_2$,
$\;|(T_{b_1}\A)\cap(T_{b_2}\A)\cap[1,N]|\le\lfloor (N+1)/\operatorname{lcm}(b_1,b_2)\rfloor$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$T_b$ is injective and $T_b(a)\le N \iff a\le(N+1)/b$, giving the first claim.
An element of the intersection satisfies $x+1=b_1a_1=b_2a_2\le N+1$, hence is
one below a multiple of $\operatorname{lcm}(b_1,b_2)$; multiples of $L$ in
$[1,N+1]$ number exactly $\lfloor(N+1)/L\rfloor$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[overlap load]\label{prop:overlap}
Let $\displaystyle S(B)=\sum_{2\le b_1<b_2\le B}\frac{\gcd(b_1,b_2)}{b_1b_2}$.
Then $\;S(B)=\frac{1}{\pi^2}(\log B)^3+O((\log B)^2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using $\gcd(m,n)=\sum_{d\mid\gcd(m,n)}\varphi(d)$,
\[
\sum_{m,n\le B}\frac{\gcd(m,n)}{mn}
=\sum_{d\le B}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d^2}\,H_{\lfloor B/d\rfloor}^{\,2},
\qquad
\sum_{d\le B}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d^2}=\frac{6}{\pi^2}\log B+O(1),
\]
and $H_{\lfloor B/d\rfloor}^2=(\log(B/d))^2+O(\log(B/d))$; integrating the
quadratic against the logarithmic density gives $(2/\pi^2)(\log B)^3/3$
before halving to ordered pairs $m<n$.
\end{proof}

Bonferroni on Propositions~\ref{prop:L1}--\ref{prop:overlap} yields a
deterministic lower bound for unions of affine images whose positive part
collapses once $(\log B)^3/\pi^2\gtrsim\delta\log B$. Even under the optimistic
ansatz $\F(M)\approx\delta M$, images of a dense low-scale region therefore
cannot certify density growth: pairwise overlap mass is \emph{cubic} in
log-scale while image mass is only linear. Finite evidence at $X=2\cdot10^6$:
$|(3\A{-}1)\cap(5\A{-}1)\cap[1,X]|=132{,}814\le133{,}333$ --- near-tight
(99.6\%), so these overlaps are structural, not accidental.

\subsection{L2: multiplication-table cap; no one-step bootstrap}

\begin{theorem}[Erd\H{o}s 1960; quantitative form by Ford, Annals 2008]
With $M(N)=|\{ab:\ 1\le a,b\le N\}|$ distinct products,
\[
M(N)\asymp\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^{\delta_F}}(\log\log N)^{3/2},
\qquad
\delta_F=1-\frac{1+\log\log 2}{\log 2}\approx0.08607 .
\]
\end{theorem}

Ford counts products over the full square $[1,N]^2$; restricting to any
$S\times S$, $S\subseteq[2,N]$, only shrinks the set, and $ab\mapsto ab-1$ is a
bijection on values. Hence:

\begin{corollary}[obstruction]\label{cor:L2}
For every $S\subseteq[2,N]$:
$|\{ab-1:\ a,b\in S\}\cap[1,N^2]|\le M(N)=o(N^2)$.
One closure step applied to an arbitrary dense seed produces relative density
$o(1)$ at height $N^2$: no implication
$\F(N)\ge\delta N\Rightarrow F(N^2)\ge\Phi'(\delta)N^2$ follows from the
closure rule alone. Density at height $N^2$ must be built through
$\sim\log N$ intermediate scales.
\end{corollary}

This kills the naive forms of interval-coverage and product-set bootstrapping.

\subsection{L3: complement propagation along primes}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:L3}
If $p\notin\A$ is prime then $pq-1\notin\A$ for every prime $q$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $pq-1\in\A$ then $pq=ab$ with $a,b\in\A$, $a,b\ge2$; the only factorizations
of the semiprime $pq$ into two factors $\ge2$ are $(p,q)$ and $(q,p)$, forcing
$p\in\A$. \end{proof}

Anchor: $11\notin\A$, since $12=2\cdot6=3\cdot4$ and $4\notin\A$ (5 prime),
$6\notin\A$ (7 prime). All $9{,}592$ numbers $\{11q-1:\ q\le10^5\text{ prime}\}$
are outside $\A$ (finite evidence). Every family produced this way has
counting function $O(N/\log N)$: complements generated by propagation have
density $0$ and cannot obstruct $d(\A)>0$; route 2 yields structure, not a
zero-density mechanism.

\subsection{L4: the 2-chain test and prime rigidity}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:L4}
For $m\ge2$: $\;2m-1\in\A$ iff $m\in\A$ or some divisor pair $(a,b)$ of $2m$
with $\{a,b\}\ne\{2,m\}$ satisfies $a,b\in\A$. In particular, if $p\ge5$ is
prime, then $p\in\A\iff qp-1\in\A$ for every prime $q\ne p$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from the divisor-pair characterization applied to $2m$: its pairs
are $(2,m),(m,2)$ and $(d,2m/d)$ over further divisors; for prime $p$, all
pairs of $2p$ and of $qp$ involve $p$ itself.
\end{proof}

\subsection{L5: powers of two (finite evidence)}

Exact memoized recursion on divisor pairs gives, for $k\le40$:
\[
2^k\in\A \iff k\in\{1,3,9,15,17,21,23,25,27,29,33,35,39\}.
\]
No even $k>0$ occurs; for odd $k$, $3\mid 2^k+1$ and membership cascades
through $(2^k+1)/3$ with unbounded certificate depth --- e.g.
$512\in\A$ via $513=3\cdot171$, $172=2\cdot86$, $87=3\cdot29$, $30=2\cdot15$,
$16=2\cdot8$, $9=3\cdot3$ (depth $\ge5$). A shallow hand analysis claiming
$\{1,3\}$ only was falsified by this computation: no bounded-depth local rule
captures $\A$.

\subsection{The GAP}

The single statement blocking all routes:
\begin{quote}
no method is known to lower-bound the count of $n\le X$ for which $n+1$ has a
divisor pair in $\A\times\A$ beyond the $O((\log X)^2)$ families of
Lemma~B; positive density requires $\ge cX$ such $n$.
\end{quote}
Status after this iteration: \textbf{STILL OPEN}, with sharpened obstruction
theory.
